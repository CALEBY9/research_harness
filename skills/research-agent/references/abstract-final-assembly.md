# Sparse-evidence abstract checks

Use this reference for an abstract with a small evidential basis, such as one
quantitative contrast and a few supporting observations. It does not prescribe
sentence count, sentence order, output language or word range. Follow the user's
requested genre and the target journal/school requirements.

- Preserve the treatment, comparator, duration, replication, sampling, measured
  quantities, numerical results and supplied uncertainty. State each where it
  serves the abstract; do not repeat design details to argue that they are credible.
- Keep content, stock, accumulation, storage and sequestration distinct. Do not
  replace a measured quantity with a stronger unmeasured one, even to disclaim it.
- Concurrent indicators do not by themselves establish a mechanism. Do not add
  significance, causality or mechanism claims unsupported by the supplied data.
  Do not introduce an unclaimed mechanism merely to deny that it was tested.
- Remove paraphrases of the same result, generic design-defense language and
  speculation added merely to fill an approximate word target. If the evidence
  cannot support the requested length, report the shortfall outside the abstract
  rather than invent or repeat material.
- Include any limitation needed to avoid materially misleading the reader.
  Ordinary scope can often be expressed by precise population, depth, time,
  treatment and measurement terms. Preserve adverse and conflicting findings.
- End with the strongest supported conclusion or bounded implication. A routine
  future-work list is optional, not a required final sentence.
  Keep the measured quantities explicit instead of recasting them as broad
  benefits. Once the study scope and descriptive status are clear, omit generic
  tested-condition or no-significance/no-mechanism disclaimers unless required
  by the author, journal, or an interpretation-changing limitation.

The historical sparse SOC example used seven sentences and 160-210 English
words. Those numbers describe that example only; they are not acceptance criteria
for another abstract. Keep verification notes outside clean prose.
