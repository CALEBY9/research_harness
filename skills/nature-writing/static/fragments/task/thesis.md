# Thesis and dissertation content

Use the author's current outline, discipline and school requirements. Reuse the
existing chapter index, manuscript and project state. Do not impose a fixed
five-chapter plan or treat a thesis as an expanded journal article.

The writing core still owns evidence, claim strength and targeted revision.
Adapt its argument and paragraph planning to the requested chapter:

- Connect the overall research question to each study/chapter, its evidence and
  its contribution to the thesis. Reuse an approved plan without another pause.
- Give shared background and methods a clear home; subsequent chapters refer
  back and explain substantive differences. Do not merge different populations,
  sampling units, scales, definitions or methods merely to make prose uniform.
- Retain derivations, method choices and analysis detail needed to understand
  the work. Journal main-text/SI compression rules do not automatically apply.
- Make the general discussion synthesize the chapters' joint answer, tensions,
  boundaries and implications, rather than concatenate chapter conclusions.
- For chapter integration or a substantive revision, check the affected
  cross-chapter promises, terminology, numbers, references and conclusions
  against the current full document. Do not rewrite unaffected chapters.

Use any existing terminology and evidence records; do not create a second set
of ledgers. Missing results stay explicit drafting placeholders outside a final
submission claim. A limitation cannot be repaired by inventing a study.

`nature-polishing` handles wording-only work. `paper_format` owns school layout,
DOCX/LaTeX mechanics and rendered QA, including its existing Master Thesis Studio
adapter. Invoke that adapter for artifact production/formatting when requested;
its historical writing template does not override the author's thesis plan.
